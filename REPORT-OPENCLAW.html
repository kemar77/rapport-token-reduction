1 · Who you are

Ten things we learned from your own words

1. "Consulting" is an undercount — you run four businesses in one

Your spec says the system "supports personal administration, health and safety consultancy, drone services, South African football charity work, security monitoring and professional document production", and asks for "separate permissioned knowledge profiles" for each. We take this seriously: that is four memory contexts, and the separation is your design — we build it as specified.

2. The human guardrail is your idea, in writing

"Always have a human in loop to ensure agents don't make decisions without human consent" — your gameplan, parallel agents

Your spec operationalizes it ("Send emails / contact leads — Approval", "Book travel / spend money — Approval", "Never automatically install software, rotate credentials, disable accounts or delete data without an authorised policy or approval"). We are aligned before the first call — this is trust design, not a sales pitch.

3. You want three things at once — and that's solvable

"Set it up for me" on 8 of 12 solutions, learn "done with me, live", hands off "teach me", tools at "$0 free only". Run head-on, somebody loses. The resolution is sequencing, not a discount: each module is one product that delivers both — we drive the install, you keep the step-by-step tutorial forever. DFY outcome, DWY skill.

4. You named the product yourself: "Not enough skills"

Your #1 bottleneck, declared while using Claude Code every day. A daily user who still feels under-skilled is exactly who skill-transfer tutorials are for — not black-box software. And since your learning budget is live sessions, every module's payback accelerates.

5. Your definition of success is repairability, not magic

"A workable AI system that is easy to manage and knowledge to fix when it does wrong" — your 90-day goal, verbatim

"Knowledge to fix when it does wrong" is the strongest possible ally of the tutorial model. Every module ends with the system running and a "when it breaks" section. You are explicitly refusing dependency on us — we respect that in the design.

6. The charity grant cycle is your first measurable win

Your verbatim ask: "Research for funding proposals for charity in South Africa and share in pdf". Your spec sharpens it: "identify South African football-development grants, sponsors, deadlines, eligibility and required documents". Grants have deadlines — a built-in scoreboard. It also chains two of your agents in one flow: research (A2) feeding a document (A4).

7. A 60-day revenue clock is already ticking

"First revenue: Within 60 days." The module order below is built for it: first digest week 3, first register week 4, grant pipeline week 6. It also disqualifies slow-payback starts — SEO and video come after day 60, on purpose.

8. Price sensitivity is about tools, not expertise

"$0 free only" monthly, but no stated cap on one-time purchases. Today you pay "Up to $50/month". The wedge is arithmetic: the first module routes that spend to free models — a $150 module that cancels a $50/month bill pays for itself in about three months.

9. Your spec is unusually mature — this is a delivery job, not discovery

You already wrote acceptance tests ("Every task has an ID, owner, status…"), a 5-phase delivery plan, an approval matrix, and 5 open decisions. Our quote answers your phases — it does not re-invent them.

10. "Not sure / several" machines — local-first fits, one server follows

Several machines suit the free local stack. The always-on night agent is what eventually justifies one small server — and only once Phase 1 demonstrably runs.

5 · Risks

Named before you find them

R1 — OAuth is the real mountain

Gmail/Drive/Calendar scoped access is the most delicate build item. Mitigation: read-only first, drafts labeled, send only on approval, tokens revocable — your §6 asks for exactly that. Slack is scheduled in M3/M8 rather than promising a date.

R2 — Four businesses, four memories, one brain

Cross-contamination — drone pricing in a grant letter, charity data in a consultancy file — is the likeliest silent failure. M5 builds the four permissioned profiles before heavy research starts, on your own taxonomy.

R3 — The $0 trap

"$0 free only" holds through weeks 1-6. The server is the one real monthly cost (~$6-12/mo); one surprise invoice later costs more trust than the truth costs now — so here it is.

R4 — We are planning against unknowns

Volume "Not sure yet", ideal client undefined, hardware uncertain. The plan optimizes your stated primary use ("Save me time") and re-checks at the week-4 checkpoint before M6-M10 are committed.

R5 — Approval friction can eat the time it saves

Your policy is approval-heavy and your goal is time. If every action pings you mid-day, the guardrail becomes the burden. Remedied by your own design: batch approvals into the morning brief with one-tap yes/no — the human-in-loop costs minutes, not interruptions.
