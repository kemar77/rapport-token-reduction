· The honest budget — how "free" actually works

The hybrid reality: free-first, not free-forever

Our own system runs free most of the time thanks to automatic routing across free frontier-adjacent models (community-served models like LongCat, GLM, DeepSeek — plus fully local ones via Ollama). When a task demands it, it switches to a paid model for that one job. This is not a promise — it is how our own company operates today.

What this means for your "$0 free only" budget — said honestly:

  • Free models are sometimes slower or occasionally saturated — timing is not guaranteed.
  • Quality varies by model and by day; the routing picks the best available, and M1 teaches you to steer it.
  • A high-stakes job (a grant letter, a client document) may warrant a paid model once — costing cents or a few dollars, never a subscription.

We do not sell the dream of "0 everywhere, always." We deliver a system that seeks the free route first and knows when to switch. That is the honest version — and the only one we know how to build.

5 · Risks

Named before you find them

R1 — OAuth is the real mountain

Gmail/Drive/Calendar scoped access is the most delicate build item. Mitigation: read-only first, drafts labeled, send only on approval, tokens revocable — your §6 asks for exactly that. Slack is scheduled in M3/M8 rather than promising a date.

R2 — Four businesses, four memories, one brain

Cross-contamination — drone pricing in a grant letter, charity data in a consultancy file — is the likeliest silent failure. M5 builds the four permissioned profiles before heavy research starts, on your own taxonomy.

R3 — The $0 trap

"$0 free only" holds through weeks 1-6. The server is the one real monthly cost (~$6-12/mo); one surprise invoice later costs more trust than the truth costs now — so here it is.

R4 — We are planning against unknowns

Volume "Not sure yet", ideal client undefined, hardware uncertain. The plan optimizes your stated primary use ("Save me time") and re-checks at the week-4 checkpoint before M6-M10 are committed.

R5 — Approval friction can eat the time it saves

Your policy is approval-heavy and your goal is time. If every action pings you mid-day, the guardrail becomes the burden. Remedied by your own design: batch approvals into the morning brief with one-tap yes/no — the human-in-loop costs minutes, not interruptions.
