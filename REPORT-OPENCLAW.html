5 · The budget reality

How "$0" holds up, line by line: free-first, not free-forever

A token is a small chunk of text, about 3/4 of a word. AIs read and write tokens, and you pay per token. Where "revocable tokens" appears later, "token" means an access key, not a chunk of text.

Our own system runs free most of the time. It routes automatically across free models almost as strong as today's frontier (community-served ones like LongCat, GLM, DeepSeek, plus fully local ones through Ollama). When a task genuinely needs it, the system switches to a paid model for that one job. We run our company this way today. The routing is not a pledge; it is our normal.

What "$0" means here, precisely

$0 in recurring software subscriptions: your current Claude Pro-type bill is the target. It is not zero infrastructure and not zero consumption. Two real line items, told here and now:

  • Server from Phase 4: once the always-on night agent exists, its host costs $6-12/mo from week 7. Before that week, $0.
  • Paid-API reserve: $5-10/mo budgeted for the rare heavy job on a paid model (a grant letter, a client document). Priced by consumption; most months it will sit unused.

And three caveats on free models themselves:

  • Free models are sometimes slower or occasionally saturated; timing is not guaranteed.
  • Quality varies by model and by day; the routing picks the best available, and M1 teaches you to steer it.
  • A high-stakes job (a grant letter, a client document) may warrant a paid model for that one call; that is what the reserve above budgets.

We do not sell "0 everywhere, always". What we build seeks the free route first and switches when it should. That is the version we can stand behind, because it is the one we live on.

9 · Risks

Named before you find them

R1: OAuth is the real mountain

Gmail/Drive/Calendar scoped access is the most delicate build item, and its verification takes calendar time (§6). Mitigation: read-only first, drafts labeled, send only on approval, tokens revocable. Your §6 asks for exactly that. Slack is scheduled in M3/M8 rather than promising a date.

R2: Four businesses, four memories, one brain

Cross-contamination (drone pricing in a grant letter, charity data in a consultancy file) is the likeliest silent failure. M5 builds the four permissioned profiles before heavy research starts, on your own taxonomy.

R3: The $0 trap
